# Research and compare

Use this skill when a decision depends on external evidence or a structured comparison of options.

## Process

1. **Frame the question.** State the decision or question, scope, constraints, and what would make an answer useful. Identify candidate options and evaluation criteria; tailor criteria to the decision.
2. **Gather evidence.** Prefer primary sources such as official documentation, specifications, original research, and direct implementation evidence. Check publication or update dates when freshness matters. Cite sources beside the claims they support and note meaningful gaps or conflicts.
3. **Verify when useful.** For implementation questions, inspect or run a small, representative proof of concept when practical. Report the environment, scope, and result; do not treat a narrow test as proof of general behavior.
4. **Compare.** Evaluate relevant factors such as maintenance, complexity, cost, compatibility, risks, and tradeoffs against the stated criteria. Do not force irrelevant dimensions or false precision.
5. **Separate claim types.** Label supported facts, hypotheses or assumptions, and recommendations clearly. Explain uncertainty and distinguish evidence from inference.
6. **Recommend.** Choose **ADOPT**, **TEST**, or **REJECT**. Explain the recommendation against the criteria, identify material caveats, and state what evidence or condition would change it.
7. **Report concisely.** Give the answer first, followed by the key comparison, supporting evidence, and unresolved questions. Keep enough source detail for another person to verify important claims.

## Completion

The result answers the framed question, compares relevant options using traceable evidence, distinguishes facts from inference, and gives a justified recommendation with uncertainty visible.
